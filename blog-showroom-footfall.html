How Furniture Showrooms Get More Footfall Without Discounting

A sale fills the showroom for a weekend. Then it trains your customers to wait for the next one. Here's how to get more people through the door while keeping your margin.

Published 29 September 2026

When a furniture showroom goes quiet, the reflex is a sale. It works, briefly. People who were already thinking about buying come in a bit sooner, the weekend looks busy, and then things go quiet again, only now your regulars know that if they wait long enough, there'll be another 20% off.

Discounting doesn't create demand. It pulls demand forward and pays for it out of your margin. The showrooms that stay busy all year give people a reason to visit that has nothing to do with price.

Give past customers a reason to come back

The people most likely to visit your showroom this month are people who've bought from you before. They know where you are, they trust your quality, and a lot of them are due another purchase: a new room, a move, a sofa that's done its ten years. Most showrooms never contact them after the sale.

You don't need a discount to bring them back, you need a reason. A new collection that suits what they bought last time. A preview evening before a range goes on the floor. A note that a matching piece to their dining table has just arrived. Personal, specific and timed around what they actually bought beats a blanket "SALE NOW ON" every time.

Make the visit something worth booking

"Pop in any time" is easy to put off. "Book a 30-minute design consultation" gives people a date in the diary and something to show up for. Offering booked appointments, whether a room-planning session, a fabric consultation or a home visit, turns a vague "we should go and look" into a committed visit. It also tells you who's coming and what they're after before they walk in, so the conversation starts halfway to a sale.

Win the "near me" search

When someone searches "furniture shop near me", they've already decided to visit somewhere. The only question is where. Your Google Business Profile does more work here than your website: accurate opening hours, recent photos of the actual showroom floor, and a steady flow of genuine reviews. Most independents set it up once and never touch it again, which makes it one of the easiest places to beat the competition down the road.

Advertise the visit, not just the product

Local Meta ads work best for showrooms when they sell the experience of coming in: see it in real light, sit on it, get honest advice. Keep the targeting to people who can realistically visit and take delivery, and give them a clear next step, like booking a visit, rather than a generic "Shop Now". Then retarget the people who engaged but didn't book, because a furniture decision rarely happens on the first look.

Borrow other people's customers

Plenty of local businesses talk to your ideal customer right before they need furniture. Estate agents and lettings teams deal with people moving house. Interior designers and kitchen fitters work with people redoing rooms. A simple referral arrangement, or a welcome offer for their clients, puts you in front of new buyers at the exact moment they're furnishing, and it costs nothing until it works.

Follow up every enquiry, fast

Footfall isn't only about new people. It's also about not losing the ones who already asked. An enquiry form filled in at 9pm that gets a reply two days later is a visit you've probably lost. A quick response with an easy way to book a time turns more of the interest you already have into people on your showroom floor.

The short version

Discounts buy one busy weekend. Reasons to visit build a showroom that stays busy: past customers invited back personally, visits people can book, a Google profile that wins local searches, ads that sell the experience, and local partners sending buyers your way. None of it costs you margin.
