What to Actually Post (Beyond Product Photos)

Most home and garden brands run out of ideas after "new stock in" and a discount code. Here's a practical list of what actually works, and why each format earns its place.

Published 11 September 2026

We've written before about why founder-led, real content outperforms generic product photography — the short version is that buyers are scrutinising harder for signs of a real business behind the account, not softer. But knowing you need more than product shots doesn't answer the actual question most owners get stuck on: fine, but post what, exactly?

Here's a working list of formats that consistently perform for independent home, garden and lifestyle brands, organised by what they're actually doing for you.

Content that builds trust before the sale

  • Real installs and deliveries — the actual process, not a staged version of it. Shows competence and gives a considered buyer a preview of what working with you looks like.
  • Before/after transformations — a real space, a real result. One of the highest-performing formats across every home and garden category because it's difficult to fake and directly answers "what will this actually look like."
  • Materials and sourcing — where something comes from, why it was chosen, what makes it different from a cheaper alternative. Justifies price without ever saying "we're not the cheapest" out loud.
  • Customer walkthroughs — an actual customer talking through their space or their decision, on camera or in their own words. Carries more weight than any brand-written copy because it isn't coming from the business.

Content that shows expertise without selling

  • Mistakes to avoid — the wrong measurement, the wrong finish for a certain room, the thing most people get wrong. Genuinely useful content earns attention and trust simultaneously, and it's the easiest format to make without feeling like an ad.
  • How-it's-made or how-it-works — the craft or process behind a product. Interesting on its own merits, and it quietly reinforces quality without a single sales line.
  • Comparisons — this material versus that one, this style versus that one. Positions you as the advisor helping someone choose, not the seller pushing one option.

Content that shows the people, not just the business

  • A decision explained — why you stock what you stock, why you turned down a supplier, why you built something a certain way. Reveals judgement, which is what a buyer is actually trying to assess.
  • Behind-the-scenes moments — the workshop, the van, the showroom before opening. Costs nothing extra to capture and is the one thing a competitor genuinely cannot copy, because it's specific to your business.
  • A founder or team member on camera — doesn't need to be polished or scripted. A real person talking is worth more than another product carousel, because it's the fastest way to answer "who am I actually trusting with this."

Where product content still belongs

None of this means stop posting products — it means product content works best once some trust already exists, not as the only thing a stranger ever sees from you. A retargeting audience who's already seen proof, process, and real people responds to a clean product shot completely differently to a cold audience who's never seen anything else. Sequence matters: earn the trust first, then let the product do the closing.

The actual constraint isn't ideas, it's consistency

Most businesses that struggle with content don't lack formats — they lack a system for capturing it regularly. A single afternoon at a delivery, an install, or in the showroom can produce weeks of the formats above if it's planned rather than left to whenever someone remembers to film something. The businesses that post well aren't more creative, they've just made capturing content a routine part of the work rather than an afterthought.
